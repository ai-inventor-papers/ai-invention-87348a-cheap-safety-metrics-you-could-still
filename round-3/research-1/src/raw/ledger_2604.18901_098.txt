Abstract
Aligned language models refuse harmful instructions, but the representations
through which they recognise such instructions are less well characterised
than the behaviours they produce.
Harmful intent is linearly separable from
residual-stream activations across 12 models spanning four architectural families
(Qwen2.5, Qwen3.5, Llama-3.2, Gemma-3) and three alignment variants (base,
instruction-tuned, abliterated), with parameter scales from 0.5B to 1.3B and a
within-family scale extension to 9B on Qwen3.5. A direction fitted from 100 la-
belled examples per class via Soft-AUC optimisation reaches mean effective AU-
ROC 0.982 and TPR@1%FPR 0.797, generalises to three held-out harm bench-
marks and a hard-benign control, and matches its instruction-tuned counterpart
within ±0.003 AUROC in abliterated variants from which the refusal mechanism
has been removed. The supervised strategies all exceed AUROC 0.96, but their
