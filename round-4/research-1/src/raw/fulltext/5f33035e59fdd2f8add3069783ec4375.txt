# Has This Checkpoint Been Abliterated? A Two-Signal Audit and Its Failure Map

Gabriel Hurtado  Affiliation: Moonsong Labs 

###### Abstract

Can a platform tell, before deployment, whether an open-weight checkpoint has had its refusal mechanism stripped? Runtime guards cannot: they score generations, not the artifact. We combine two cheap internal signals, a reference-anchored activation _refusal-gap_ and a _weight-recovery energy_ of the base-to-candidate weight difference, into a threshold-free checkpoint audit. The two are negatively correlated and label-complementary: the gap supplies refusal-specificity and the weight energy supplies recall. On a 273273-checkpoint registry spanning Qwen, DeepSeek-distilled Qwen, Llama, and Gemma, their zz-sum separates 5757 public abliterations from 3737 benign fine-tunes, merges, and instruction-tunes at AUROC 0.950.95, significantly above either signal alone (0.840.84, 0.900.90), and a Youden-calibrated threshold transfers to held-out families at balanced accuracy 0.890.89 (FPR 0.110.11), missing only 44 of 5757. We then map two failures, in order of severity: a _spoofed reference_ evades both axes with no training (Δ​W=0\Delta W{=}0, ρ=1\rho{=}1 by construction), and a white-box owner trains a checkpoint past the threshold while it stays guard-unsafe and coherent. The audit is effective triage, not tamper-proofing: it presumes an attested reference, and its claims are bounded by the registry we evaluate it on.  
  
---  
  
## 1 Introduction

Open-weight models advertised as _uncensored_ or _abliterated_ appear within days of major base releases, produced by orthogonalizing the residual stream against a refusal direction (Arditi et al., 2024; Labonne, 2024) or newer per-layer and multi-direction variants (p-e-w, 2025; Piras et al., 2025). Platforms and governance need a checkpoint-level question answered before deployment (Shevlane et al., 2023; Reuel et al., 2024): has this artifact had its refusal mechanism removed? Runtime guards cannot answer it: they score generations, not the model, and black-box access alone is insufficient for rigorous audits (Casper et al., 2024).

We calibrate two complementary internal signals already proposed for adjacent tasks (activation separation (Messenger, 2026), weight-difference monitoring (Zhong & Raghunathan, 2025)) into an effective checkpoint audit, then map where it nonetheless fails.

Contributions.

  1. 1.

An effective checkpoint-triage detector. Standardizing and summing a reference-anchored activation refusal-gap and the WeightWatch weight-recovery energy gives a threshold-free score at AUROC 0.950.95, significantly above either signal alone, that transfers across unseen families (leave-one-family-out balanced accuracy 0.890.89). The signals are complementary: the gap is refusal-specific but misses multi-direction removal; the weight energy catches it but over-flags benign low-rank edits (§4).

  2. 2.

A failure map. The detector is still not tamper-proof: a spoofed reference defeats it with no training, and a white-box owner trains a checkpoint past it while it stays guard-unsafe (§5).

  3. 3.

A public registry with benign-edit negatives. A 273273-checkpoint registry (abliterations across four families plus benign fine-tunes, merges, and instruction-tunes as hard negatives) on which the audit is evaluated (§4).




## 2 Related Work

Runtime guards score generations, not the artifact (Casper et al., 2024); an activation probe alone is evadable and a weight-difference monitor alone cannot prove removal (only editing). We combine the two as label-complementary signals and, more importantly, chart where even the combination fails.

AMS (Messenger, 2026) flags collapse of harmful/benign activation separation; we benchmark its Tier-1 absolute-σ\sigma scan; its Tier-2 mode compares a candidate against a trusted baseline. Our activation axis differs in scoring a reference-anchored ratio of the harmful-minus-benign gap (not a fingerprint-integrity match). Empirically, Tier-1 absolute thresholding misses an abliterated Qwen2.5-1.5B sibling that Tier-2 reference-verification catches: reference-anchoring, not absolute thresholding, surfaces the edit. WeightWatch (Zhong & Raghunathan, 2025) monitors the top singular directions of the base–candidate weight difference. Jain et al. (2024) show safety fine-tuning is a low-rank, null-space MLP edit, so a low-rank weight delta signals safety editing in either direction; we use this weight-recovery energy for recall and rely on the activation gap for refusal-specificity. CWAC (coupled weight and activation constraints) (Peng et al., 2026) proves, for defense, that weights or activations alone are insufficient; we show the same for detection, which our adaptive attack then defeats.

Refusal spans a cone of directions (Wollschläger et al., 2025; Piras et al., 2025; Pan et al., 2025), and the choice of direction affects what is captured (Siu et al., 2025); this bounds a single-direction gap (§5).

Bailey et al. (2024) obfuscate activations past latent monitors, and McGuinness et al. (2025) train models to evade even unseen activation monitors; we extend this line from activations to the joint weight+activation checkpoint setting, against representation-level defenses such as circuit breakers (Zou et al., 2024) and weight-level tamper-resistance (Tamirisa et al., 2024).

Concurrent work uses the same refusal-vector primitive for provenance fingerprinting (Xu & Sheng, 2026) and detects abliteration as a direction-agnostic deviation in activation-geometry fingerprint (Coslett, 2026); we differ in scoring a reference-anchored gap ratio to detect refusal removal rather than lineage or structural drift, fusing it with the weight axis, and demonstrating the detection-aware evasion that both leave as an open question (Coslett, 2026; Xu & Sheng, 2026). These recipes are widespread (Young, 2025); a simple refusal-hardening defense exists (Abu Shairah et al., 2025), which we use as a control (§B).

## 3 Method

### Setup.

An auditor holds a candidate McM_{c} and a trusted, attested reference MbM_{b} (base or sibling, with pinned lineage). A behavioral oracle (Qwen3Guard (Qwen Team, 2025), think-traces stripped) labels whether a checkpoint is uncensored, supplying ground-truth labels rather than serving as the detector.

### Two complementary signals.

From a harmful/benign contrast set we form per-layer difference-of-means refusal directions r^ℓ\hat{r}_{\ell} on MbM_{b} (representation reading; Zou et al., 2023a; Arditi et al., 2024). The _activation gap_ is

| ρ=gap⁡(Mc)gap⁡(Mb),gap⁡(M)=1|ℬ|​∑ℓ∈ℬ⟨μhℓ​(M)−μbℓ​(M),r^ℓ⟩,\rho=\frac{\mathrm{gap}(M_{c})}{\mathrm{gap}(M_{b})},\qquad\mathrm{gap}(M)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{B}}\big\langle\mu^{\ell}_{h}(M)-\mu^{\ell}_{b}(M),\,\hat{r}_{\ell}\big\rangle, |   
---|---|---  
  
where μhℓ,μbℓ\mu^{\ell}_{h},\mu^{\ell}_{b} are the mean last-token activations at layer ℓ\ell over harmful/benign prompts and ℬ\mathcal{B} is the mid-stack layer band (one scalar per model). It is near 11 for an intact candidate and falls toward 00 as refusal is removed; anchoring to MbM_{b} lets one score transfer across families of different scale. It is refusal-specific but bounded to a single direction, so it misses refusal removed along a non-canonical direction (§5). The _weight-recovery energy_ is

| E1=1|𝒲|​∑m∈𝒲σ12​(Δ​Wm)∑iσi2​(Δ​Wm)∈[0,1],Δ​Wm=Wbm−Wcm,E_{1}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{W}|}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{W}}\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}(\Delta W_{m})}{\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}^{2}(\Delta W_{m})}\in[0,1],\qquad\Delta W_{m}=W_{b}^{m}-W_{c}^{m}, |   
---|---|---  
  
where 𝒲\mathcal{W} is the set of attention-output (o_proj) and MLP-down (down_proj) weight matrices from each layer in the mid-stack band ℬ\mathcal{B}, and σi\sigma_{i} are the singular values of Δ​Wm\Delta W_{m} (the rank-1 energy fraction of the edit, band-averaged; WeightWatch, Zhong & Raghunathan, 2025). It fires on any low-rank weight edit, so it catches multi-direction removals the gap misses, but also responds to aggressive benign edits and cannot by itself distinguish refusal _removal_ from _addition_ (App. B). The two are complementary: ρ\rho supplies refusal-specificity, E1E_{1} supplies recall.

### Combined detector.

The two signals are negatively correlated across the audited set (Pearson r=−0.41r{=}{-}0.41) and label-complementary, so we zz-standardize each on a reference population and sum: s⁡(Mc)=z⁡(−ρ)+z⁡(E1)s(M_{c})=z(-\rho)+z(E_{1}). The score is threshold-free; the standardization is transductive (it uses the reference population’s mean/SD), so a deployable audit fixes the operating point by Youden’s JJ on a calibration set of attested references, whose transfer to unseen families we report in §4. (An absolute, standardization-free subspace variant DcombD_{\mathrm{comb}} is defined in App. A; the zz-sum transfers at least as well, so we report it.)

## 4 Results

We sweep public abliterations across Qwen, DeepSeek-distilled Qwen, Llama, and Gemma. Scoring one checkpoint means downloading its full weights and running both the generation-based guard and the activation-and-weight detector, so coverage is compute-bound on our single 6464 GB workstation: of the 273273-checkpoint registry we fully processed 7171 (those with both a Qwen3Guard label and detector output), as many as the budget allowed rather than a curated subset. The 5757 uncensored among them, plus a separate 3737 benign edits, form the 9494-checkpoint evaluation set (full attrition in App. D; the second guard covers the 5959 with cached generations). Hardware and decoding details are in App. D.

In-family, yes (Table 1). Excluding each test point from the reference moments leaves the in-sample AUROC unchanged (0.9480.948 leave-one-out vs. 0.9480.948 pooled), so the transductive standardization does not leak.

### Q2: Does the operating point transfer?

Yes (Table 1, held-out). Under leave-one-family-out calibration, the zz-sum reaches balanced accuracy 0.890.89 (FPR 0.110.11), above either signal alone (ρ\rho 0.780.78, E1E_{1} 0.810.81) and an absolute subspace variant (DcombD_{\mathrm{comb}} 0.820.82, App. A). Reference-anchoring is what earns the activation component its transfer: held-out detection climbs from 0.350.35 for an absolute-σ\sigma AMS Tier-1 scan, to 0.420.42 for reference-anchored AMS Tier-2, to 0.830.83 for our harmful-minus-benign anchored ratio. The weight energy E1E_{1} then supplies the recall on multi-direction recipes the gap misses (§5, Fig. 2).

Figure 2: ROC for the two signals and their zz-sum; the absolute-σ\sigma AMS Tier-1 baseline (■\blacksquare) sits well below. In-sample AUROC 0.950.95 (zz-sum) vs. 0.900.90 (E1E_{1}) and 0.840.84 (ρ\rho); the zz-sum is significantly above both (Table 1).

### Q3: Do the two signals complement each other?

Yes, and it is why the combined zz-sum is significantly more separable than either signal alone (Table 1). The single-direction gap fails in two ways. (i) When refusal is removed along several directions at once (Heretic), one difference-of-means direction captures only part of the edit. (ii) In reasoning-distilled models the refusal decision migrates into the generated chain-of-thought (Yamaguchi et al., 2025) and is suppressed only at the final tokens (Yin et al., 2025), which a last-token gap cannot see and which chain-of-thought jailbreaks exploit (Zhao et al., 2025b). Both kinds of edit stay low-rank in weight space, so E1E_{1} keeps firing and the combined detector recovers them: it misses only 4/574/57 (Fig. 1), and those 44 are ordinary abliteration variants, not the hard recipes. Auditing reasoning checkpoints on the gap alone would still need trace-level probing, an open problem.

## 5 Where the Audit Fails (Q4)

Two breaks, in order of severity: the first is architectural and needs no compute, the second needs white-box training.

### A spoofed reference defeats the audit without training.

Both signals need a trusted base, but the audit tolerates an honest mistake about which clean sibling it is: scoring an abliterated Qwen3 instruct candidate against the _pretrain_ *-Base instead of its true instruct base only drops the rank-1 weight energy from E1=1.0E_{1}{=}1.0 to 0.770.77 (still detected), because the abliteration stays the dominant low-rank component of Δ​W\Delta W despite the added instruct-tuning delta. The audit breaks only when the declared reference is _itself_ adversarial: declaring an already-abliterated sibling shrinks Δ​W\Delta W and inflates ρ\rho, evading both axes with no training (2/22/2 checkpoints, ρ=0.69/0.73\rho{=}0.69/0.73); using the candidate as its own reference evades by construction (Δ​W=0\Delta W{=}0, ρ=1\rho{=}1). This is the architectural limit: the audit is only as strong as its base attestation, so the root of trust must be a publisher-signed checkpoint hash or hardware-attested lineage, not an uploader-nominated base (App. E).

### A white-box adversary trains past it.

A Qwen2.5-1.5B fine-tune optimized against the held-out detector objective (App. D) drives the combined score from detected to evaded (Fig. 3), an existence proof on a single family and scale (Limitations). At the evaded checkpoint (step 600600) both axes read benign (E1=0.29E_{1}{=}0.29, ρ=1.82\rho{=}1.82), yet the model is guard-unsafe on every held-out harmful prompt (Qwen3Guard 20/2020/20, [.83,1.0][.83,1.0]), non-refusing (substring refusal 0.150.15), and coherent (perplexity 180180 vs. the base’s 194194). It also keeps most base capability (MMLU 0.580.58 vs. 0.590.59; GSM8K 0.510.51 vs. 0.570.57), a small “obfuscation tax” on general knowledge and a modest one on math, comparable to what public abliterations incur (Young, 2025). Bailey et al. (2024) name this tax for activation monitors; here it appears in the joint weight+activation setting. As a design check, natural fine-tuning does not open this hole: across 66 Qwen SFT/DPO full fine-tunes E1E_{1} stays high even as the model becomes uncensored, so the adaptive hole requires targeted optimization.

Figure 3: A white-box adversary drives the combined score from above its detection threshold (detected) to well below it (evaded) over training. The annotated step-600600 checkpoint hides both axes while remaining non-refusing, coherent, and guard-unsafe (all 2020 held-out prompts).

## 6 Limitations

Three limits bound the claims. Construct validity. Ground-truth labels come from one behavioral guard and certify refusal, not harmlessness (refusal and harmfulness are separate axes; App. B). Separately, E1E_{1} fires on any low-rank edit, so it cannot alone distinguish refusal removal from other aggressive edits, including safety-hardening; the activation gap supplies the refusal-specificity, and because our benign negatives contain no hardened models the headline FPR is optimistic against that case. Transfer and calibration. The evaluation is small (n=57/37n{=}57/37), so absolute rates are imprecise, with especially wide bands on the held-out operating-point metrics; and because the score is standardized and thresholded on a reference population, cross-family transfer is only estimated by leave-one-family-out, not guaranteed for a genuinely novel architecture. Adversarial robustness. The activation gap alone is bounded to one direction (it misses multi-direction removal, which E1E_{1} recovers here but a direction-aware attacker could target jointly), and the white-box hole is a one-family, one-scale proof of concept. A trained linear probe on raw activations, a multi-family attack, same-axis or human label adjudication, and a layer-band ablation are left to future work.

### Broader impact and safeguards.

The audit helps platforms and reviewers triage redistributed checkpoints before deployment; its dual use is a detection-aware evasion recipe (novel: natural fine-tuning does not open this hole). We make the audit metric, code, and recipe available on request for reproducibility, but withhold the evaded weights and raw harmful generations and report only aggregate guard rates; because the attack modifies the owner’s own weights, it exposes no third-party vendor’s users to coordinate disclosure with (App. F).

## 7 Conclusion

For a platform triaging redistributed checkpoints, the two-signal zz-sum is a cheap and effective first filter: under an attested base it separates public abliterations from benign edits at AUROC 0.950.95 and transfers to unseen families at balanced accuracy 0.890.89, with a flag prompting review rather than rejection. Its limits are just as concrete, and an operator must hold all three at once: the audit rests entirely on the reference, so a spoofed base voids it at the root; the weight axis alone cannot tell refusal removal from other low-rank edits; and a white-box owner can train past both signals while staying uncensored. Effective triage is not tamper-proofing. Our contribution is the detector together with the map of exactly where it stops working.

## Appendix B Construct validity of the two signals

Four checks test that the activation gap and weight energy measure what we claim.

### The activation gap is not a norm artifact.

Abliteration also shrinks residual-stream norms, but recomputing ρ\rho from cosine (unit-norm) projections raises it rather than removing it (raw vs. cosine ρ\rho ranges 0.040.04–0.240.24 vs. 0.070.07–0.320.32 across two abliteration families at 4B/8B), so the harmful-minus-benign separation is directional, not a magnitude effect, and stays well below the ρ<0.5\rho{<}0.5 flag either way.

### A single direction is bounded; the union is not.

Refusal occupies a cone of directions rather than one axis (Wollschläger et al., 2025), so a single difference-of-means rr can be evaded along an orthogonal component. We confirm the bound directly: removing refusal along a direction orthogonal to r^\hat{r} drives the activation gap to ρ≈0.99\rho{\approx}0.99 (evading the activation axis), yet the weight energy still fires (E1=1.0E_{1}{=}1.0) and the checkpoint still refuses (0%0\% guard-unsafe). By contrast, canonical r^\hat{r}-aligned removal yields a genuinely uncensored model (75%75\% guard-unsafe) and collapses the gap to ρ≈0\rho{\approx}0, caught on both axes. Neither edit escapes the two-signal sum: the single-direction gap is bounded but E1E_{1} recovers the multi-direction case, which is why summing the signals catches the Heretic and reasoning recipes the gap alone misses (§5).

### The gap measures refusal, not harmfulness.

Zhao et al. (2025a) show that harmfulness and refusal are encoded along separate directions, and that adversarially fine-tuning a model to comply barely moves its internal harmfulness representation. Our refusal-gap therefore certifies whether the refusal mechanism is present, not whether a model is harmless, and the white-box attack (§5) hides only the refusal axis. A harmfulness-direction probe is complementary and could flag a checkpoint that evades the gap.

### The weight energy is direction-agnostic, so the gap supplies refusal-specificity.

E1E_{1} fires on any low-rank weight edit, in either safety direction. A safety-strengthening control makes this explicit: extended-refusal hardening (Abu Shairah et al., 2025) applied to an abliterated base re-inserts refusal (refusal rate 1.01.0 vs. 0.120.12 for the un-hardened control) yet still has E1=1.0E_{1}{=}1.0. So E1E_{1} alone cannot tell refusal removal from addition, and a negative set containing safety-hardened models would draw false positives from the weight axis; the refusal-specificity in the combined detector comes from the activation gap, and gating E1E_{1} on canonical-direction alignment (which we examined) does not fix this, since hardening is itself refusal-aligned. This is why we report the construct caveat in §5/Limitations rather than claim a standalone weight forensic, consistent with Jain et al. (2024).

## Appendix C Audit protocol

The deployable audit is a fixed procedure, summarized so it can be re-implemented and disagreement localized.

  1. 1.

Inputs. A candidate checkpoint McM_{c} and a trusted, attested reference MbM_{b} (the base or a sibling with pinned, verifiable lineage; §E).

  2. 2.

Refusal directions. On MbM_{b} only, form per-layer unit difference-of-means refusal directions r^ℓ\hat{r}_{\ell} from a fixed harmful/benign contrast set (below). They are never estimated on audited candidates, so no candidate (or held-out family) leaks into r^ℓ\hat{r}_{\ell}.

  3. 3.

Score. Compute the activation gap ρ⁡(Mc)\rho(M_{c}) and the weight-recovery energy E1​(Mc)E_{1}(M_{c}) (below), standardize each against the attested reference population, and sum: s⁡(Mc)=z⁡(−ρ)+z⁡(E1)s(M_{c})=z(-\rho)+z(E_{1}). (An absolute, standardization-free variant DcombD_{\mathrm{comb}} is in App. A.)

  4. 4.

Threshold. Fix the operating point by Youden’s JJ on the attested calibration population; the leave-one-family-out numbers in Table 1 estimate the transfer of that threshold to a family held out of calibration.

  5. 5.

Output. A flag plus the two component scores, so an over-flagged benign edit (held-out FPR 0.110.11) can be triaged by hand rather than auto-rejected: the audit is pre-deployment triage, and the deployment harm of a false positive is a manual re-review, not a blocked release.




## Appendix D Reproducibility details

### Contrast set for r^ℓ\hat{r}_{\ell}.

The refusal directions are built from a fixed set of 500500 harmful/benign prompt pairs (seed 2026052820260528; source files pinned by SHA-256). The harmful prompts are drawn from established red-teaming benchmarks: AdvBench (Zou et al., 2023b) (379379), JailbreakBench (Chao et al., 2024) (6363), and the HarmBench (Mazeika et al., 2024) validation split (5858), spanning the usual misuse taxonomies (illegal activity, cyber, violence, chemical/biological, fraud, harassment, privacy). Each harmful prompt is paired with a hand-authored, topically matched benign instruction that shares its leading verb, so the difference-of-means isolates refusal rather than topic or surface form. The same set is reused for every family; it never includes audited checkpoints.

## Appendix E Threat model

We consider three adversaries, ordered by capability. The _non-textbook recipe_ (detector unknown, no access beyond public weights, already trained) evades a component axis but is recovered by the zz-sum (the complementarity of §4); only the next two defeat the combined audit. The _reference spoofer_ (detector known, declares the reference, no compute) evades both axes with no training. The _white-box owner_ (detector known, full weights, moderate fine-tune) trains the score below threshold while staying guard-unsafe. The audit assumes an honest, attested reference; both the spoofer and the white-box owner break that assumption, which is why we treat base attestation, not the score, as the root of trust (§E).

### Attestation as the root of trust.

Both signals are only as strong as the reference. An operational deployment should therefore obtain MbM_{b} through a channel that binds weights to a verifiable lineage (a publisher-signed checkpoint hash, or hardware-attested training provenance) and reject self-supplied references, rather than accepting whatever base an uploader nominates. This converts the spoofing attack from a silent bypass into a failed attestation. A second, untested layer would check the credibility of the declared parent itself, e.g. by running the same refusal audit on the reference, which would expose an already-abliterated sibling presented as a base.

### Construct validity of the ground truth.

Our labels come from a behavioral guard, so the audit can only certify behaviorally-obvious refusal removal; a checkpoint that is internally edited but still behaviorally refusing (or one whose refusal has migrated into an unscored chain-of-thought, §5) is by construction mislabeled. This guard-anchored ground truth is also why the reasoning-distilled misses may be partly a labeling artifact rather than purely a detector failure, and it is a deliberate limit of behavioral grounding, not a claim that the guard is itself trustworthy: the audit targets the model artifact, the guard only supplies labels.

### Label robustness under a second, independent guard.

To check that the uncensored labels are not an artifact of the single primary guard, we re-judged the cached harmful-prompt generations with a second guard from a different family, Granite Guardian 3.0-2B (Padhi et al., 2024). The open refusal-judging guards (WildGuard, Llama Guard, ShieldGemma) are access-gated, so the second guard scores a different axis: whether each response is harmful, rather than whether it is a refusal. Even across both a different family and a different construct, its compromised/clean labels agree with the refusal-based primary at Cohen’s κ=0.78\kappa{=}0.78 (93%93\% raw agreement, 4/594/59 checkpoints flipped). The few disagreements fall exactly on the refusal-versus-harmfulness boundary the audit already acknowledges: three checkpoints comply without emitting guard-flagged harmful content (non-refusing but not harmful), and one reasoning checkpoint is the reverse. The substantial agreement indicates the labels are robust to the choice of guard; the residual gap is the refusal/harmfulness distinction, not guard noise.

## Appendix F Ethics and data statement

### Data.

The registry is built only from already-public model checkpoints on open repositories and from established, openly released red-teaming prompt sets (AdvBench, JailbreakBench, HarmBench); it contains no human subjects, no private or personally identifying data, and no new harmful content, so no IRB review applies. The datasheet lists, per checkpoint, the repository id, revision hash, family, recipe label, behavioral label, and scan date, with the include/exclude flag and reason, so the audited population is fully specified and auditable.

### Harmful generations.

Harmful prompts are run only to score refusal; we never release raw harmful generations, reporting only aggregate guard-unsafe and substring-refusal rates, and we withhold every abliterated and adversarially evaded checkpoint we produced.

### Dual use and disclosure.

The detector aids defenders (platform and registry operators triaging redistributed checkpoints before deployment); its dual-use surface is the white-box evasion recipe, which is novel (it is detection-aware; natural fine-tuning does not open this hole, §5), so we do not appeal to the prevalence of ordinary abliteration to excuse it. We judge release defensible on different grounds: the attack requires white-box control of a checkpoint the owner already holds, and the marginal offensive uplift is outweighed by the defensive value of a reproducible audit and a documented failure mode. For reproducibility the training recipe is given in Appendix D; we withhold the evaded weights and all raw harmful generations. Because the attack modifies weights the owner already controls and targets a defense class rather than a deployed model’s users, it exposes no third-party vendor’s users, so it does not carry the coordinated-disclosure obligation that a jailbreak against a hosted model would.
